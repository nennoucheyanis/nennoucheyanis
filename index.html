Eco Dôme
du Hamma

A graduation project proposing the redevelopment of the zoo area of Jardin d'Essai du Hamma in Algiers.

Inspired by the geometry of a snail shell, the project explores architecture as an ecosystem where geometry, climate, material and living systems interact.

01

Concept

The architectural concept is derived from the spiral geometry of a snail shell. The shell becomes both a formal and environmental strategy, generating a continuous circulation sequence while creating controlled climatic conditions for the tropical ecosystem.

02

Genesis

Starting from the bio-inspired spiral geometry of a natural shell, the design extracts a guiding curvature and merges it with a radial dome grid to generate a refined, parametric structure optimized for the tropical ecosystem.

07

Materials

Material selection directly drives the performance of the Eco Dôme. The protective envelope was selected to maximize light and thermal control for the internal tropical ecosystem, while the primary structural material was chosen for its strength, durability, and ability to span the dome's complex geometry.

01 / ETFE envelope

Lightweight and highly transparent envelope used to create the climatic interface of the dome while allowing controlled solar transmission and natural daylight.

MAT / 01
02 / Aluminium

Selected for its high resistance to coastal corrosion, this lightweight structural frame works in synergy with the ETFE envelope—reducing overall weight by up to 80% compared to glass. It offers rapid assembly, exceptional durability, and complete recyclability.

Environmental strategy

Architecture
responding to climate.

Understanding the Eco Dôme requires looking beyond its form to explore how it breathes and adapts. This section delves into the key environmental strategies, passive climate mechanics, and innovative systems designed to create a sustainable microclimate for the tropical ecosystem.

Passive system

Wind circulation, controlled openings, and adaptive envelope components work together to regulate the interior climate, preventing heat buildup from the greenhouse effect and avoiding excess humidity that causes mold.

01 / Wind circulation — Plan

Plan diagram illustrating airflow dynamics through the dome, highlighting the relationship between prevailing winds, internal circulation, and microclimatic zones.

PASSIVE / 01
02 / Air movement — Section

Sectional diagram illustrating vertical airflow driven by the stack effect, thermal stratification, and the evacuation of accumulated warm air through diaphragms at the top of the dome.

PASSIVE / 02
Variable aperture 9 blades / kinetic system
03 / Diaphragm Opening

A kinetic ventilation opening inspired by the mechanical logic of an iris diaphragm. Nine overlapping blades pivot around eccentric points to continuously modify the central aperture. When the system opens, the increased free area facilitates air movement; when it closes, the aperture is reduced to limit direct exposure and airflow.

04 / Kinetic Brise-Soleil

Interactive simulation of the kinetic brise-soleil system protecting the dome's glazed façade: three large curved blades pivot around a fixed anchor point, continuously adapting to the sun's path from dawn to dusk.

The environmental strategies are finally tested through hygrothermal simulation. The analysis is developed at two different scales: first, through a material-based comparison using WUFI, and then through an AI-assisted assessment of the complete envelope geometry.

AI-assisted / Full-envelope simulation

After identifying the most relevant material behaviours at the material scale, the analysis is extended to the complete envelope geometry.

METHODOLOGY
Due to the complexity of the complete geometry and the computational time required for a full hygrothermal simulation, an AI-assisted model was used to estimate the temperature and relative humidity behaviour of the complete envelope.

The model provides a comparative overview of the three material configurations before further design development and validation.

Between Sky and Sea

An urban design project exploring the relationship between the city, the landscape and the Mediterranean coastline.

The project develops an urban strategy based on connectivity, public space, landscape continuity and the redistribution of urban programmes.

Location
Algiers
Type
Urban Design
Scale
Urban / Territorial
Approach
Urban Strategy / Landscape / Public Space
01

Problematic

The project begins with the identification of the main urban, spatial and social issues affecting the site.

Fragmented urban connections, discontinuous public spaces and an unclear relationship between the city and the coastline create a territory in need of a coherent urban structure.

02

Concept

The concept establishes a continuous relationship between the urban fabric, public spaces and the sea.

The project seeks to reconnect fragmented areas through a sequence of public spaces and urban connections, creating a stronger relationship between the city and its landscape.

03

Urban Strategy

The urban strategy is developed through a series of complementary interventions that restructure movement, public space, landscape, and programme, aiming to overcome the strong slope to seamlessly connect the isolated upper city a high-income residential area with panoramic views—with the lower city, which primarily serves the middle class.

Rehabilitation / 02

Metal Beam
& Brick Vault Floor

Reconstruction Architectural Practice Algiers
01

EXISTING FLOOR SYSTEM

Structural principle

The existing floor consists of steel beams spanning between the supporting walls, with brick vaults constructed between the beams.

The steel beams support the brick vaults, while the supporting walls transfer the loads to the existing structure. Additional floor layers are built above the vaults.

This section establishes the structural principle used to understand the construction photographs that follow.

02

SITE PROTECTION

Pedestrian protection

Before demolition began, protective measures were installed along the street and façade to reduce the risk of falling materials, debris and construction elements reaching pedestrians and the surrounding public space.

Temporary weather protection

A temporary TN40 weather protection system was installed to limit rainwater infiltration while the roof and terrace structure were exposed during the works.

This temporary measure helped protect the existing structure and the spaces below from further water penetration.

Structural shoring

Temporary shoring was installed to support the existing structure and safely transfer loads while the floor system was exposed and reconstructed.

This bracing remained in place throughout the demolition and reconstruction phases, ensuring the stability of the surrounding structure until the new floor system was completed.

03

STRUCTURAL SURVEY

Investigation

Localized exploratory openings were carried out to expose the existing floor layers and investigate the condition of the structural elements.

These openings provided direct access to the steel beams, brick vaults and surrounding construction layers.

The investigation followed a simple sequence: exploratory opening → observation → assessment → decision.

04

STRUCTURAL ASSESSMENT

Condition assessment

The exposed structural elements were assessed to determine their condition and identify the elements that could be preserved or required replacement.

Particular attention was given to the condition of the steel beams, especially in areas affected by moisture and water infiltration.

05

DETERIORATED STEEL BEAMS

Corrosion damage

Several existing steel beams showed significant deterioration caused by prolonged exposure to moisture and water infiltration.

Advanced corrosion had resulted in material and section loss. Elements whose structural condition was no longer satisfactory were therefore identified for replacement.

Sound structural elements were retained wherever their condition allowed.

06

SELECTIVE DEMOLITION

Deconstruction

The existing floor was dismantled progressively, section by section, rather than being demolished as a whole.

The upper layers were removed, debris was evacuated and deteriorated structural elements were progressively taken out.

This approach allowed the sound portions of the existing structure to be preserved.

07

STRUCTURE EXPOSED

Structural exposure

The floor and terrace layers were progressively removed and the debris evacuated, exposing the structural system beneath.

Once exposed, the remaining structure could be inspected, cleaned and prepared for the reconstruction phase.

08

SUPPORT RECONSTRUCTION

Load-bearing support

Dismantling of damaged joists and supply of replacement elements. The new joists, fabricated to the exact cross-sectional dimensions of the original members, were hoisted to the floor level and reinstated into their original bearing recesses.

The bearing support was leveled and prepped prior to setting the new steel beams.

09

ANTI-CORROSION TREATMENT

Steel protection

An anti-corrosion treatment was applied to the new steel elements to provide protection against future corrosion.

09

NEW STEEL BEAMS

Structural replacement

The deteriorated steel beams were replaced with new steel profiles positioned according to the existing structural logic.

The new elements were installed on the prepared supports to recreate the original floor system.

11

BRICK VAULT CONSTRUCTION

Structural reconstruction

New brick vaults were constructed between the steel beams, reproducing the original structural principle identified during the initial survey.

The vaults recreate the traditional floor system while restoring the continuity between the steel beams and the masonry structure.

12

SAND & LIME LAYER

Floor preparation

A layer of sand mixed with lime was distributed over the reconstructed floor surface to provide levelling and prepare the assembly for the subsequent floor layer.

13

PERIPHERAL REINFORCEMENT

Edge reinforcement

Reinforcement was prepared around the perimeter of the floor to complete the connection between the reconstructed floor system and its surrounding supports.

14

SCREED APPLICATION

Floor finishing layer

A screed was applied over the prepared floor assembly to provide a level and continuous surface for the subsequent finishing layers.

This layer completes the preparation of the floor before the final floor finishes are installed.

15

TERRACE RECONSTRUCTION

Continuity of the system

The same structural principle was subsequently reproduced for the terrace, with new steel profiles positioned according to the existing structural logic and brick vaults reconstructed between them.

The terrace therefore continues the same construction system rather than introducing an unrelated structural method.
